A friendship quiz is a set of questions about your life, habits, and preferences that your friends answer to see how well they know you. Done well, it becomes part game, part conversation starter, and part excuse to laugh at the answers your friends get hilariously wrong. Here is a simple process for building one from scratch.

Pick a theme before you write anything

The best quizzes have a point of view. Instead of mixing whatever comes to mind, choose a lane: childhood memories, food opinions, travel dreams, or everyday habits. A theme keeps the quiz focused and makes the results feel like a real portrait of you. Ten to fifteen questions is the sweet spot. Fewer feels thin, and more starts to feel like homework.

Write questions with one clear answer

Vague questions make for frustrating quizzes. "What is my favorite color?" works because it has a single true answer. "What would I do on a free Saturday?" only works if you offer four specific options to choose from. Mix easy wins with a few tricky ones so casual friends stay confident while your closest friends get to show off what they know.

Make the wrong answers fun too

The decoy options matter as much as the correct ones. A funny wrong answer turns a simple quiz into entertainment, even for people who score badly. If your group has running jokes, work one or two into the options. The quiz should sound like you, because it is a small portrait of your personality.

Choose how friends will take it

You can run a quiz in a group chat, read questions aloud at a party, or use an online quiz maker that scores answers automatically. If you want to create a friendship quiz for friends without building the scoring yourself, an online tool handles the questions, the points, and the shareable results link.

Friendship itself has been studied for centuries. The Wikipedia article on friendship is a good reminder of why small rituals of connection like this one matter more than they seem.

Share it and compare results

Send the finished quiz to your group chat on a slow evening and watch the scores roll in. The real fun starts when everyone compares answers and argues about the ones they missed. Keep the tone light, crown a winner, and let the lowest scorer choose the theme for the next round.
